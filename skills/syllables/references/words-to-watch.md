# Words to watch

These lists help you anticipate where a count or stress might surprise you, so you can choose words well. They are not a way to count: the check is always the authority, and it knows the accent in the musician's settings. If the check disagrees with anything here, trust the check.

## Contents

3. Contractions and informal forms

## 1. Words with more than one sung count

**A weak vowel before "r" or "l" that can drop** (the stressed syllable stays):

| Word | Counts | Shorter form sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| every | 2 or 3 | ev-ry |
| different | 2 or 3 | diff-rent |
| family | 2 or 3 | fam-ly |
| history | 2 or 3 | hist-ry |
| memory | 2 or 3 | mem-ry |
| favourite / favorite | 2 or 3 | fave-rit |
| general | 2 or 3 | gen-ral |
| natural | 2 or 3 | nat-ral |
| camera | 2 or 3 | cam-ra |
| several | 2 or 3 | sev-ral |
| separate (adjective) | 2 or 3 | sep-rit |
| evening | 2 or 3 | eve-ning |
| interest | 2 or 3 | in-trest |
| heaven | 1 or 2 | heav'n |

**A vowel before "r" that can merge into one syllable or stay as two:** fire, hire, tired, higher, liar, desire, hour, our, power, flower, tower, shower (each 1|2).

**Others:** real (1|2). In British English, an unstressed "-ia" or "-ual" can be sung as one syllable or two, as in "actual".

Variable words are useful: they let one line fit two slightly different melodies, or let the musician choose between a smooth and a precise delivery. Make sure matching lines in different verses use the same choice.

## 2. Words that are easy to miscount

- **One syllable, though they can look or feel longer:** world, girl, curl, earl, film (in most accents), squirrel (one in some US speech, two in UK).
- **Fewer syllables in everyday speech than the spelling suggests:** chocolate, comfortable, vegetable, temperature, literally, actually, probably, business, Wednesday.
- **Two syllables that sometimes get counted as one:** being, quiet, science, lion, poem, diamond (two or three), violent (two or three).

For all of these, the check decides; if the musician sings the word differently, ask and re-check with their pronunciation in mind.

## 3. Contractions and informal forms

| Full form | Short form | Syllables |
|---|---|---|
| I am | I'm | 2 → 1 |
| do not | don't | 2 → 1 |
| will not | won't | 2 → 1 |
| can not / cannot | can't | 2 → 1 |
| it is | it's | 2 → 1 |
| you are, we are, they are | you're, we're, they're | 2 → 1 |
| I would, I had | I'd | 2 → 1 |
| we will | we'll | 2 → 1 |
| let us | let's | 2 → 1 |
| going to | gonna | 3 → 2 |
| should have, could have, would have | should've, could've, would've | 2 → 2 (no saving) |
| did not, is not, could not | didn't, isn't, couldn't | usually 2 → 2 |
| it will, that will | it'll, that'll | 2 → 2 |
| want to, got to, kind of | wanna, gotta, kinda | 2 → 2 |
| running, loving | runnin', lovin' | 2 → 2 |

Contractions change the register as well as the count. "I'm" is conversational; "I am" is formal or emphatic. Match the musician's voice.

## 4. Function words that weaken

These are normally unstressed in a line, and sound weak or reduced in connected speech: a, an, the, and, but, or, of, to, for, from, at, in, on, as, than, that (in "the one that left"), is, are, was, were, be, been, can, could, would, should, have, has, had, do, does, some, my, your, his, her, our, their, them, us, me.

They become stressed when the meaning demands it ("I said THE song, not a song", "it's YOUR call"). Otherwise, keep them off strong beats, long notes and melodic high points, where they would sound oddly important.

## 5. UK and US differences

**Count:** the -ary, -ery and -ory endings are full syllables with a secondary stress in US English and weak (often shortened) in UK English:

| Word | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| cemetery | CEM-e-ter-y | CEM-e-try |
| February | FEB-ru-ar-y | FEB-ru-ry, or FEB-ru-ar-y |
| secretary, dictionary, territory, military, necessary | full -ar-y / -or-y ending | weaker ending, often shortened in speech |

**Stress:**

| Word | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| garage | ga-RAGE | GA-rage |
| ballet | bal-LET | BAL-let |
| address (noun) | AD-dress or ad-DRESS | ad-DRESS |
| laboratory | LAB-ra-to-ry | la-BOR-a-to-ry |
| controversy | CON-tro-ver-sy | CON-tro-ver-sy or con-TROV-er-sy |

**Vowels that change rhyme and sound but not count:**
- "a" in bath, path, dance, can't, laugh, glass: long "ah" in Southern British English, short "a" in US English and in Northern English accents.
- "o" in hot, lot, bother: a rounded short "o" in UK English, closer to "ah" in US English, so "bother" rhymes with "father" in US English but not in UK English.
- Final "r" in car, more, fear: silent in UK English, pronounced in US English.

**Different words or forms:** maths (UK) and math (US), aluminium (UK, 5 syllables) and aluminum (US, 4 syllables). Use the musician's word.
