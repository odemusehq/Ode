# Words to watch

These lists help you anticipate where a count or stress might surprise you, so you choose words well the first time. They are not a way to count: the syllable check is always the authority, and it knows the accent in the settings. If the check disagrees with anything here, trust the check, and use the count it gives in the result line.

## 1. Words with more than one sung count

**A weak vowel that can drop** (the stressed syllable stays):

| Word | Counts | Shorter form sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| every | 2 or 3 | ev-ry |
| different | 2 or 3 | diff-rent |
| family | 2 or 3 | fam-ly |
| history | 2 or 3 | hist-ry |
| memory | 2 or 3 | mem-ry |
| favourite / favorite | 2 or 3 | fave-rit |
| general | 2 or 3 | gen-ral |
| natural | 2 or 3 | nat-ral |
| camera | 2 or 3 | cam-ra |
| several | 2 or 3 | sev-ral |
| separate (adjective) | 2 or 3 | sep-rit |
| evening | 2 or 3 | eve-ning |
| interest | 2 or 3 | in-trest |
| heaven | 1 or 2 | heav'n |

**A vowel before "r" that can merge into one syllable or stay as two:** fire, hire, tired, higher, liar, hour, our, power, flower, tower, shower (each 1 or 2); desire (2 or 3, on its last syllable).

**Others:** real (1 or 2); quiet, science and lion (1 or 2 in UK English, 2 in US English); in British English an unstressed "-ia" or "-ual" can be one syllable or two, as in "India" or "actual".

Variable words are useful: they let one line fit two slightly different melodies, or let the musician choose between a smooth and a precise delivery. Matching lines in different verses use the same choice, and the result line names it: "fits with 'every' sung 'ev-ry'".

## 2. Words that are easy to miscount

- **One syllable, though they can look or feel longer:** world, girl, curl, earl, film.
- **Two syllables that some singers make one:** squirrel (two in the check; some US singers make it one).
- **Fewer syllables in everyday speech than the spelling suggests:** chocolate, comfortable, vegetable, temperature, literally, actually, probably, business, Wednesday. The check lists the shorter counts it accepts.
- **Two syllables that sometimes get counted as one:** being, poem; and diamond and violent, which can be two or three.

For all of these the check decides. If the musician has told you they sing a word differently, use their count and say so in the result line.

## 3. Contractions and informal forms

| Full form | Short form | Syllables |
|---|---|---|
| I am | I'm | 2 → 1 |
| do not | don't | 2 → 1 |
| will not | won't | 2 → 1 |
| can not / cannot | can't | 2 → 1 |
| it is | it's | 2 → 1 |
| you are, we are, they are | you're, we're, they're | 2 → 1 |
| I would, I had | I'd | 2 → 1 |
| we will | we'll | 2 → 1 |
| let us | let's | 2 → 1 |
| going to | gonna | 3 → 2 |
| should have, could have, would have | should've, could've, would've | 2 → 2 (no saving) |
| is not, could not | isn't, couldn't | 2 → 2 (no saving) |
| did not | didn't | usually sung as 2; the check also allows 1 |
| it will, that will | it'll, that'll | it'll 1 or 2; that'll 2 |
| want to, got to, kind of | wanna, gotta, kinda | 2 → 2 |
| running, loving | runnin', lovin' | 2 → 2 |

Contractions change the register as well as the count. "I'm" is conversational; "I am" is formal or emphatic. Match the musician's voice.

## 4. Function words that weaken

These are normally unstressed in a line and sound weak in connected speech: a, an, the, and, but, or, of, to, for, from, at, in, on, as, than, that (in "the one that left"), is, are, was, were, be, been, can, could, would, should, have, has, had, do, does, some, my, your, his, her, our, their, them, us, me. The stress check weakens them too, including at the end of a line ("said to me" ends on a weak syllable).

They become stressed when the meaning demands it ("I said THE song, not a song", "it's YOUR call"). Otherwise keep them off strong beats, long notes and melodic high points, where they sound oddly important.

## 5. UK and US differences

**Count:** the -ary, -ery and -ory endings are full syllables with a secondary stress in US English. In UK English the ending is weak and some words lose a syllable:

| Word | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| cemetery | CEM-e-ter-y (4) | CEM-e-try (3) |
| February | FEB-ru-ar-y (4) | FEB-ru-ry (3) or FEB-ru-ar-y (4) |
| secretary, dictionary, territory, military, necessary | full -ar-y / -or-y ending | the check gives the full count too; UK singers often shorten it in speech |

**Stress:**

| Word | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| garage | ga-RAGE | GA-rage |
| ballet | bal-LET | BAL-let |
| address (noun) | AD-dress or ad-DRESS | ad-DRESS |
| laboratory | LAB-ra-to-ry (4) | la-BOR-a-to-ry (5) |
| controversy | CON-tro-ver-sy | CON-tro-ver-sy or con-TROV-er-sy |

**Vowels that change rhyme and sound but not count:**
- "a" in bath, path, dance, can't, laugh, glass: a long "ah" in Southern British English, a short "a" in US English and in Northern English accents.
- "o" in hot, lot, bother: a rounded short "o" in UK English, closer to "ah" in US English, so "bother" rhymes with "father" in US English but not in UK English.
- Final "r" in car, more, fear: silent in UK English, pronounced in US English.

**Different words or forms:** maths (UK) and math (US); aluminium (UK, 4 or 5 syllables) and aluminum (US, 4). Use the musician's word.
