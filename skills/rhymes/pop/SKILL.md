# Rhymes in pop

Pop rhymes help the listener predict and sing along. A good pop chorus feels inevitable: the ear knows roughly where it's going, and the arrival still feels satisfying. The rhymes skill covers the loop, the kinds of rhyme, schemes and checking; this file adds what is specific to pop.

## Choruses

- **Land the chorus on strong rhymes.** The ends of chorus lines, above all the title line, are where perfect rhymes pay off. Near rhymes are welcome in verses and increasingly common in choruses, but a chorus of only weak rhymes can feel like it never lands.
- **Or let the title stand alone.** Three rhymed lines and then an unrhymed title line (AAAB) make the title jump out because it breaks the pattern.
- **Stay on one or two rhyme sounds.** A chorus that keeps returning to the same vowel works like a hook in itself and is easier to sing back.
- **Rhyme the title with something unexpected,** not its most obvious partner. If the obvious rhyme is the only one that fits, leave the title unrhymed instead.
- **Repetition is allowed.** Repeating the title makes an identical rhyme with itself; in a pop chorus or post-chorus that is the hook doing its job, not a weak rhyme. Don't "fix" it.

**Example (original), AAAB with the title unrhymed:**

> *Can't go up and can't come down* (7)
> *Watching all the lights in town* (7)
> *Each new circle comes back round* (7)
> *I'm in a holding pattern* (7)

The rhyme check (UK) calls "down" / "town" perfect and "round" near (the extra "d"), so the first three lines chime on one sound; the title breaks the pattern and sticks. All four lines have seven syllables, so the melody can repeat its rhythm.

## Pre-choruses

- Internal rhymes and short rhymed phrases build momentum and pull toward the chorus.
- An unsettled scheme (three rhymed lines, or an unrhymed last line) increases the lift; save the strongest closing rhyme for the chorus.

## Post-choruses

- Echo the title's vowel so the two hooks feel connected.
- One or two repeated words are enough; rhythm and vowel matter more than rhyme here.

## Rhyme words on long notes

In pop the rhyme word often lands on the longest note of the line, so its vowel has to sing:

- Open vowels hold beautifully: "go", "slow", "day", "far", "down". The vowel can be open without the pair being stale; still check the pair against the over-used list.
- Short vowels with hard endings ("bit", "stuck", "pop") cut the note off. Put them on short notes, or choose another rhyme sound for long ones.
- The singability check reports a closed vowel on a long or high note; fix it before reporting.

## Freshness in a repeated chorus

A chorus is heard four or more times, so a stale pair is exposed every time. If an over-used pair sits in a chorus line you change, make the rest of the line surprising, or re-end the partner line so the pair isn't needed, and make the change in every chorus.

## Verify, on top of the rhymes skill's checks

- The chorus lands on a strong rhyme as the rhyme check labels it, or leaves the title line unrhymed on purpose.
- The chorus stays on one or two rhyme sounds.
- Rhyme words on long notes pass the singability check.
- No over-used pair sits in a chorus line you wrote unless you kept it on purpose and the result line says so.
