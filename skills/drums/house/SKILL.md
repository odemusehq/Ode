# House drums

Only what is specific to house. Grid notation, velocities and the General MIDI note map are in the drums skill.

## Tempo and phrasing

- Usually around 118–128 BPM in 4/4: deep house towards the lower end, tech house around 124–128.
- Everything happens in 8-, 16- and 32-bar phrases. Elements enter, leave and change on phrase boundaries (bars 17, 33, 49…), because DJs and dancers count in phrases.

## The core groove

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Kick    X . . . X . . . X . . . X . . .
Clap    . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Hat op  . . o . . . o . . . o . . . o .
Shaker  x g x g x g x g x g x g x g x g
```

- **Kick** on every beat, steady velocity, its tail short enough to leave room for the bass.
- **Clap or snare** on 2 and 4; a clap layered with a snare, or two claps slightly apart, for width.
- **Open hat** on every off-beat — the lift that makes house move.
- **Shaker or closed hats** in sixteenths with a strong accent pattern; this is where the swing lives.

## Swing and percussion

- Swing the sixteenths gently, around 54–58%, on hats, shakers and percussion; leave the kick and clap straight.
- Add percussion — congas, bongos, rimshots, toms, cowbell — in syncopated one- or two-bar patterns that sit between the kicks. Vary or swap them every 8 or 16 bars.
- Deep house leans on softer kicks, more swing and more percussion; tech house on a punchy kick, rolling percussion and fewer, tighter sounds; Afro house on layered, interlocking percussion.

## Variations

- Drop the kick for the last beat or bar of a phrase, so the next downbeat lands harder.
- An extra kick on the "a" of 4, or a clap on the "a" of 4, at phrase ends.
- A ride on the off-beats or in quarters for peak sections.
- Open or close the hat's decay over a phrase to raise or lower intensity.

## Arrangement for the dance floor

- **Intro and outro**: 16–32 bars of kick, hats and percussion with few other elements, so a DJ can mix in and out. Bring elements in every 8 or 16 bars.
- **Breakdown**: remove the kick (and often the bass) for 8–32 bars; the chords, vocal or a pad carry it.
- **Build**: 4–8 bars of rising tension — a clap or snare roll, risers, a filter opening — into the drop.
- **Drop**: the kick returns on the downbeat, often with a crash or an impact, and the full groove.

A four-bar clap build into a drop, velocity rising from about 60 to 120:

```
Bar 1 (quarters)    X . . . X . . . X . . . X . . .
Bar 2 (eighths)     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Bar 3 (sixteenths)  x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x
Bar 4 (sixteenths)  x x x x x x x x x x x x . . . .
```

The silent last beat before the drop makes the returning kick hit harder.

## Kick and bass

- In many house styles the bass plays on the off-beats between the kicks; the kick owns the beat and the bass answers it. Agree the bass rhythm with the bass skill.
- Ducking the bass and pads with the kick (sidechain compression) gives the familiar pumping; that is a mixing choice, covered by the mixing skill.

## Check (house-specific)

- The kick is on every beat, unless it is dropped on purpose at a phrase end.
- Changes happen on 8- or 16-bar boundaries.
- Intro and outro suit DJ mixing if the track is meant for clubs.
- The kick's tail and the bass don't overlap.
