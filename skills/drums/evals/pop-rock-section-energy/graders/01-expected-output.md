The result matches this description: Ode writes a different pattern for each section rather than a
louder copy of the same beat: a sparser verse (for example closed hats or cross-stick and a simple
kick), a building pre-chorus (denser hats, toms or a snare build, or the kick dropped for tension),
and a fuller chorus (open hats or ride, more kick, a crash on the downbeat, possibly a layered snare
and clap or a tambourine). It adds small fills at phrase ends and a bigger fill or a drop-out into
the chorus that lands on the downbeat, uses varied velocities and ghost notes where they suit, and
writes everything as grids or note lists with bar lengths. It leaves the existing drum clip
untouched and puts the new parts in new clips for comparison, explains each section's choices
briefly in musical terms, and, if it offers three versions, labels one as outside the musician's
usual style.
