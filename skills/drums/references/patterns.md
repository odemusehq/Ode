# Drum patterns

Grids as in the drums skill: one bar of sixteenths in 4/4, `X` accent, `x` hit, `g` ghost note, `o` open hi-hat, `.` rest. These are common, generic patterns to start from; shape them to the song, the bassline and the vocal.

## 1. Pop-rock: verse and chorus

Verse — cross-stick (side stick, note 37) instead of the snare, closed eighth hats, a simple kick:

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Stick   . . . . x . . . . . . . x . . .
Kick    X . . . . . . . X . . . . . . .
```

Chorus — full snare, sixteenth hats with accents, an open hat into the next bar, a busier kick, and a crash on the first downbeat:

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     X x x x X x x x X x x x X x o .
Snare   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Kick    X . . x . . . . X . x . . . . .
```

Same tempo, clearly different energy: subdivision, snare sound, kick density and the open hat all change.

## 2. Half-time

The snare moves to beat 3; the groove feels half as fast and heavier.

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Snare   . . . . . . . . X . . . . . . .
Kick    X . . . . . . x . . X . . . . .
```

Useful for bridges, breakdowns and heavy choruses in rock, pop and hip-hop.

## 3. Shuffle

Grid in triplet eighths: twelve steps per bar, three per beat (the dots in the header are the second and third notes of each triplet).

```
        1 . . 2 . . 3 . . 4 . .
Hat     x . x x . x x . x x . x
Snare   . . . X . . . . . X . .
Kick    X . . . . . X . x . . .
```

The hat plays the first and third note of each triplet: the classic shuffle. A swung sixteenth grid at around 66% gives a similar feel.

## 4. Funk with ghost notes

Sixteenth hats (accent the eighths, soften the in-between notes), a syncopated kick, and ghost notes around a strong backbeat.

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     X x X x X x X x X x X x X x X x
Snare   . . . g X . . g . g . . X . g .
Kick    X . x . . . . . . . X . . x . .
```

Keep the ghost notes soft (20–40) or the groove turns cluttered.

## 5. R&B slow groove

Around 65–80 BPM, swung sixteenths (55–60%), space for the vocal.

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     x . x x x . x . x . x x x . x .
Snare   . . . . X . . . . . . g X . . .
Kick    X . . . . . x . . x . . . . . .
```

A finger snap or rim layered with the snare, and a late-placed snare, suit this feel.

## 6. Four-on-the-floor

A kick on every beat, the backbeat on 2 and 4, open hats on the off-beats. The base of disco and much dance music (the house sub-skill has the house detail).

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat cl  x x . x x x . x x x . x x x . x
Hat op  . . o . . . o . . . o . . . o .
Snare   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Kick    X . . . X . . . X . . . X . . .
```

The closed hat leaves out the steps where the open hat sounds; with both in one choke group they behave like a real hi-hat.

## 7. Two-step breakbeat

The basic drum and bass groove at around 170 BPM: kick on 1 and on the "and" of 3, snare on 2 and 4.

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Snare   . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Kick    X . . . . . . . . . X . . . . .
```

At this tempo keep hats to eighths or a light sixteenth shaker, and add ghost snares sparingly.

## 8. Fills

One-beat fill on beat 4 (end of a four-bar phrase):

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     x . x . x . x . x . x . . . . .
Snare   . . . . X . . . . . . . x x x X
Kick    X . . . . . . . X . x . . . . .
```

Two-beat tom fill, high to low (section change):

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Hat     x . x . x . x . . . . . . . . .
Snare   . . . . X . . . x x . . . . . .
Tom hi  . . . . . . . . . . x x . . . .
Tom mid . . . . . . . . . . . . x x . .
Tom low . . . . . . . . . . . . . . x x
Kick    X . . . . . . . X . . . X . . .
```

Other transitions:

- **Drop-out**: stop the drums after beat 2 of the last bar, or play only a snare pickup on the "and" of 4, so the chorus downbeat lands in contrast.
- **Kick pickup**: an extra kick on the "a" of 4, leading into the next downbeat.
- **Snare build** over two or four bars: quarters, then eighths, then sixteenths, velocity rising.
- Land every fill on the next downbeat with a kick and, at section changes, a crash.

## 9. Varying a loop

- **A A A B**: three bars of the groove, then a variation or a fill in bar 4.
- **A A′ A A″**: small changes in bars 2 and 4 (an extra kick, an open hat, a dropped hat), a bigger change at bar 8.
- Open hat on the last off-beat of bars 2 and 4 to mark the half-phrase.
- Remove the hats, or the kick, for the last bar before a new section.
- Keep the change at the end of the phrase so the downbeat of the next phrase feels like a fresh start.
