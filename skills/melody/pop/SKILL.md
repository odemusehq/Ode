# Pop melody

Only what is specific to pop. The loop (read, decide, change in place, verify, let them hear it, report in one line), the general checks and the craft are in the melody skill, with the same conventions (scientific pitch, lengths in beats of 4/4).

## Common pop approaches

- **The hook early and often.** The chorus states its hook within its first two bars; the title usually sits there, on the last line, or both.
- **Repetition as a feature.** A pop chorus often holds only two melodic ideas, each repeated. What looks too simple on paper usually feels right on the second listen.
- **A clear lift** from verse to chorus, in register, rhythm or both.
- **Mostly syllabic, conversational word setting**, with short melismas on vowels or the title.

## Contrast over a looped progression

Many pop songs loop the same two to four chords through verse and chorus, so the melody (with the arrangement) has to create the sections. When more contrast is wanted, try one or more of these; a low chorus or minimal change may be intentional:

- **Register** — chorus tessitura a third to a fifth above the verse's.
- **Rhythm** — verse in shorter notes, often starting after beat 1 or with sixteenth-note pickups; chorus in longer notes starting on or just before the downbeat. The reverse works too: a sparse verse and a rhythmic, chant-like chorus.
- **Phrase length** — one- or two-bar phrases in the verse, two- or four-bar phrases in the chorus, or the opposite.
- **Density** — words per bar.
- **Contour** — a static or wave-like verse; a chorus that arches to a peak.

Example over one loop, `| Am | F | C | G |` at 100 BPM.

Verse — low, repeated notes, starting on beat 2, speech-like:

```
| Am | r(1) C4(½) C4(½) C4(½) B3(½) A3(1) |
| F  | r(1) A3(½) A3(½) C4(1) A3(1) |
| C  | r(1) E4(½) E4(½) E4(½) D4(½) C4(1) |
| G  | D4(2) r(2) |
```

Chorus — a fourth higher, starting on the downbeat, a long–short–long motif:

```
| Am | A4(1½) G4(½) E4(2) |
| F  | A4(1½) G4(½) F4(2) |
| C  | G4(1½) E4(½) G4(1) C5(1) |
| G  | B4(3) r(1) |
```

The verse spans A3–E4 and ends open on D4. The chorus spans E4–C5; its rhythm repeats in bars 1–2, varies in bar 3 to climb to the peak, and falls to an open B4 so the loop pulls back to Am. Same chords, clearly different sections.

## Pre-chorus

- Lift with a rising contour, a fragmented motif, or rhythmic acceleration (shorter phrases, notes closer together).
- End open, on a held note or a cut-off that leaves a short gap — often a beat or two of silence — before the chorus downbeat. The gap makes the chorus land harder.
- Keeping its peak below the chorus can reserve a later high point; a descending or low chorus can create a different arrival.

## Placing the hook and the title

- Put the title on the first line of the chorus, on the first strong beat or on the held note, and repeat it at the end of the chorus.
- Common chorus layouts by line (letters are melodic ideas): A A B A, A B A B, or A A A B (the hook three times, then a closing line).
- Give the hook a rhythm absent from the verse, so it stands out the moment it arrives.

## Post-chorus and instrumental hooks

- A post-chorus repeats a short hook — a chant, a wordless vowel line, or a repeated fragment of the title — over the chorus chords or an instrumental drop.
- Keep it singable on first listen: narrow range (a third to a fifth), strong rhythm, lots of repetition.
- In dance-pop the chorus may hand over to an instrumental hook (a synth lead or a chopped vocal). It is still a melody: write it with the same motif rules, often on pentatonic notes so it fits every chord of the loop.

## Pop phrasing habits

- **Conversational verses**: repeated notes, speech rhythm, syncopated sixteenths at mid tempos.
- **Pentatonic hooks**: the major and minor pentatonic scales avoid half steps, so hooks built on them are easy to sing and sit over most chords of a loop.
- **Anticipations**: arriving on the "and" of 4 before the barline is the default pop push into a new bar.
- **The last chorus** often adds a higher alternative line, a long held note or ad-libs above the hook; keep the original hook recognisable underneath (the vocals skill writes those parts).

## Sub-style tendencies

Loose tendencies: use them only to choose a take; what the artist wants comes from the artist.

| Sub-style | Melody tendencies |
|---|---|
| Dance-pop | short repeated phrases, chant-like hooks, strong syncopation, narrow-range post-chorus |
| Pop ballad | longer phrases, wider range, held notes, the biggest note saved for the bridge or final chorus |
| Synth-pop | rhythmic, often repeated-note verses; the hook shared between voice and synth |
| Indie-pop | narrower range, talk-sung verses, modal colours (Dorian, Mixolydian), less obvious peaks |
| Pop R&B | syncopation, colour tones such as 9ths, short melismas on key words, runs saved for ad-libs |

## Trying something outside the usual style

On Again, or when the artist's habit is the thing holding the song back, try the opposite of the habit: a low, rhythmic, chant-like chorus for an artist who belts high; a sparse verse of long notes for one whose verses are always wordy; a hook that starts off the beat for one whose hooks always start on the downbeat. Explain the changed effect when useful; a requested departure from earlier habits needs no automatic warning.

## Checks for pop

Review these alongside the melody skill for the chosen pop direction; they are options, not a quality threshold.

| Check | What to review |
|---|---|
| Hook | consider whether early placement and a strong, long or high note help the requested hook; withholding it is also an option |
| Contrast | assess the requested contrast in register, rhythm, phrase length, density or contour without counting mandatory differences |
| Pre-chorus | review its chosen handover; an open ending or lower peak is useful for some arcs, not required |
| Post-chorus | review recognisability and singability; a fifth-wide range and two repeats are starting points |

## Result lines

- "Pre-chorus now climbs by step and stops a beat early · ends open on the 5th, below the chorus peak."
- "Post-chorus hook on 'oh' over the drop, three notes inside a fourth · in key over every chord of the loop."
