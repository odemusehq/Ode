# Forms and timing

Example forms with typical section lengths, what often plays in each section, and an energy rating from 1 to 10. They are common starting points to adapt: the song's own story, hook and the artist's habits decide. A form that breaks these patterns on purpose is often the most memorable.

## Verse–chorus song

| Section | Bars | Often | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | The hook or chord pattern on one or two instruments | 3 |
| Verse 1 | 8–16 | Vocal with a reduced band; drums light or absent | 4 |
| Pre-chorus | 4–8 | Rising melody, chords moving faster, a build in the last bars | 5 |
| Chorus 1 | 8–16 | Full band, the hook, the widest and brightest so far | 7 |
| Verse 2 | 8–16 | Like verse 1 but changed: new counter-melody, different drum pattern, extra percussion | 5 |
| Pre-chorus 2 | 4–8 | As before, maybe bigger | 6 |
| Chorus 2 | 8–16 | As chorus 1 plus one new element | 8 |
| Bridge | 8 | Contrast: new chords, a different rhythm, or stripped back | 4 or 6 |
| Final chorus | 8–16, often repeated | The biggest: harmonies, doubles, octave lift, extra percussion | 9 |
| Outro | 4–8 | The hook winding down, a cold ending or a fade | 3 |

## Verse–chorus with post-chorus

A post-chorus of 4–8 bars after each chorus repeats a short hook (a vocal chop, a chant, an instrumental riff) and keeps the energy high before dropping into the next verse. It suits songs where the chorus melody is long and a shorter, simpler hook needs its own spot.

## 32-bar AABA

| Section | Bars | Often |
|---|---|---|
| A | 8 | The main melody and title line |
| A | 8 | The melody again with new lyrics |
| B (bridge) | 8 | Contrasting melody and chords |
| A | 8 | The main melody returns, often ending on the title |

Common in older popular song, jazz standards and many ballads. Instrumental solos often take a whole AABA chorus.

## Club track

Dance music is built in 8, 16 and 32-bar phrases so DJs can mix tracks together and dancers feel changes coming. Change something at least every 8 bars and make larger changes every 16 or 32.

| Section | Bars | Often | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 16–32 | Drums and percussion, building; little harmonic content so DJs can mix | 4 |
| Groove | 16–32 | Bass and main elements enter | 5–6 |
| Breakdown | 16–32 | Drums and low end out; pads, chords, vocal or the hook revealed | 3 |
| Build | 8–16 | Risers, snare repeats, filter rising, low end removed at the end | 7 |
| Drop or main section | 32 | Full groove and hook | 9 |
| Second breakdown | 16 | Shorter or different from the first | 4 |
| Build | 8–16 | Bigger than the first | 7 |
| Second drop | 32 | The first drop plus a new element or variation | 10 |
| Outro | 16–32 | Elements leave until drums remain, for mixing out | 4 |

## Build and drop form

Shorter and more song-like than a club track, often with a vocal.

| Section | Bars | Often | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8 | A filtered hint of the drop or the vocal hook | 3 |
| Verse | 8–16 | Vocal over a reduced beat | 4 |
| Build | 8 | Rising tension, the vocal hook repeated | 6–7 |
| Drop | 16 | The main instrumental hook, full low end | 9 |
| Verse 2 | 8–16 | Changed from verse 1 | 5 |
| Build | 8 | Bigger than the first | 7 |
| Drop 2 | 16 | Variation of drop 1 | 10 |
| Outro | 8 | Winding down or a cold end | 3 |

## Rap verse and hook form

| Section | Bars | Often |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | The beat's main loop, sometimes filtered |
| Hook | 8 | The most melodic and repeated part |
| Verse | 16 (sometimes 8, 12 or 24) | The beat with room for dense lyrics; drops and switches in the beat mark key lines |
| Hook | 8 | As before |
| Verse | 16 | Beat variation: new percussion, a removed element, a changed bass pattern |
| Hook | 8 | Often with added layers |
| Outro | 4–8 | The loop or a final hook |

Dense lyrics need space: keep the beat's busiest elements out of the vocal's range during verses, and use drops (removing the beat for a beat or a bar) to spotlight important lines.

## Instrumental and ambient pieces

Without a vocal, interest comes from slow change: layers entering and leaving every 8–16 bars, gradual changes in filtering, texture and harmony, and a clear peak somewhere past the middle. Keep a recognisable motif returning so the piece has an anchor.

## Duration tables

In 4/4, **seconds = bars × 240 ÷ BPM**; **bars per minute = BPM ÷ 4**.

| BPM | Bars per minute | 8 bars | 16 bars | 32 bars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70 | 17.5 | 27.4 s | 54.9 s | 1:50 |
| 80 | 20 | 24.0 s | 48.0 s | 1:36 |
| 90 | 22.5 | 21.3 s | 42.7 s | 1:25 |
| 100 | 25 | 19.2 s | 38.4 s | 1:17 |
| 110 | 27.5 | 17.5 s | 34.9 s | 1:10 |
| 120 | 30 | 16.0 s | 32.0 s | 1:04 |
| 124 | 31 | 15.5 s | 31.0 s | 1:02 |
| 128 | 32 | 15.0 s | 30.0 s | 1:00 |
| 140 | 35 | 13.7 s | 27.4 s | 0:55 |
| 160 | 40 | 12.0 s | 24.0 s | 0:48 |
| 174 | 43.5 | 11.0 s | 22.1 s | 0:44 |

**Bars for a target length:** bars = seconds × BPM ÷ 240. A 3-minute song at 100 BPM is 75 bars; at 128 BPM, 96 bars. Round to the nearest form made of full phrases.

**Half-time feel:** music at 140 or 174 BPM is often felt at half that tempo; the bar maths stays the same, but sections can feel twice as long, so fewer bars may be enough.

**Other meters:** use the number of beats per bar the tempo counts (for example, 3 for 3/4): seconds = bars × beats per bar × 60 ÷ BPM.
