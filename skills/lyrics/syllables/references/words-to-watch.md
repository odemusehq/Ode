# Pronunciation questions to check

These are prompts for the configured Lyricist, not a dictionary or a list of accepted model outputs. Give it the actual phrase, intended accent, notes and tempo. The singer's chosen delivery takes priority over a model estimate.

- **Reduction:** words such as every, different, family, memory or camera can compress weak vowels. State the specific reading needed rather than silently changing a count.
- **Adjacent vowels and r:** fire, hour, our, power and similar words may merge or separate in a sung phrase. Accent and delivery matter; nationality alone does not establish the reading.
- **Spelling versus sound:** words such as world, film, squirrel, poem, chocolate and comfortable can invite incorrect counting assumptions. Ask about the intended pronunciation in context.
- **Contractions:** I am / I'm can reduce a syllable; other contractions may keep their count. Check the whole phrase and register, not an unconditional subtraction rule.
- **Grammatical role:** record, present and related words can change lexical stress with their role. Interpret the sentence.
- **Function words:** articles, pronouns and auxiliaries are often weak, but can carry intentional contrast or emphasis. Do not strip stress from every line-final pronoun automatically.
- **Regional or sung variation:** dictionary, laboratory, garage and other words vary across speakers and performances. UK/US settings are starting context, not an exhaustive accent taxonomy.

Describe the pronunciation a proposed setting requires, retain ambiguity when unresolved, and review actual articulation time, stress and breath placement. A matching total does not prove the line can be sung well. Use `lyrics/syllables` and `lyrics/prosody` for the complete word-to-note plan.
