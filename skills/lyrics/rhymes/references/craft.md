# Rhyme: sound, meaning and expectation

Ask `lyricist` to assess the actual lines in their intended sung accent and context. Forward this file through `lyricist.references` when needed. Ode stores text and structured annotations; it does not run a local rhyme dictionary or phonetic engine.

## Hear the relation before naming the pattern

A perfect rhyme shares the stressed vowel and following sounds, with different preceding onsets. In common UK/US pronunciations, "home" and "stone" are perfect rhymes, not near rhymes. Spelling is not evidence: "love" and "move" do not become rhymes because they look alike.

Near rhymes can retain a vowel with different consonants, add/remove ending consonants, or use another audible resemblance. Assonance connects vowels; consonance connects consonants. Repeated words and homophones can form an intentional sonic return without becoming a different-onset perfect rhyme. Multisyllabic and internal describe extent and placement, not an automatic quality score.

Give Lyricist the actual ending words, their intended pronunciation and the preceding material. Ask what matches and differs. Do not label an arbitrary pair perfect to satisfy a requested scheme, silently alter the accent, or claim an unheard singer makes a weak pair exact.

## Labels describe this song

Ode's rhyme labels identify sound groups across the **whole song**. Reuse a letter for the same established sound; assign a new letter when a new sound appears, including in a later section. If a verse is ABAB and a chorus introduces two different sounds, its labels are CDCD, not a fresh local ABAB.

A near relationship can be annotated as such without pretending the endings are identical. Keep the actual phonetic rationale clear. Reassess affected groups against the whole song after changing an end word, including linked repetitions. The book's local section-pattern notation is not an instruction to reset Ode's global sound identities.

## Closure depends on the setting

Perfect rhyme can make a clearer arrival; looser matches can leave more open space. Neither is better by definition. Hear the combined rhyme timing, phrase lengths, syntax, melody and delivery. A final perfect rhyme need not resolve an unsettled harmony; a near rhyme can still feel conclusive in its musical setting.

AABB may create repeated local stops; ABAB can postpone the first answer; repeated A sounds may feel stable or insistently repetitive. These are patterns to audition, not fixed emotions or genre assignments. Four printed lines need not settle and five need not remain open. Ask what the established pattern led the listener to expect and what the actual arrival does.

If repeated couplets stop too often, try delaying a rhyme, changing relative stressed length or joining the pairs into a larger phrase. If a crucial thought passes unnoticed, alter the expected rhyme/length or expose its arrival. Keep the change purposeful rather than making every line surprising.

## Find choices that say something

1. Establish the line's meaning, speaker and protected material.
2. Explore words/images from that situation and several useful rhyme sounds together. Do not accept the first rhyme merely because it fits a grid.
3. Ask Lyricist for candidates whose sound relation it can explain in the intended accent. The Pattison reference offers ways to widen the search.
4. Compare complete lines for meaning, conversational credibility, voice, sung phrasing and closure.
5. Choose the useful line; revise the partner ending or use near/no rhyme if that serves the intention better. Recheck the passage and relevant repetitions.

A forced inversion, unrelated image or empty phrase is a reason to reconsider the rhyme choice. A familiar pair is not automatically wrong: a plain title, chant or recontextualised phrase may need it. Do not replace every familiar pair with the same domestic imagery or with an obscure word chosen only to appear original.

## Accent and singing

Use the musician's actual pronunciation rather than a rigid UK/US table. Regional, individual and sung readings can change a relation. When uncertainty affects the choice, state the required reading or ask for it; do not present a broad national default as verified delivery.
