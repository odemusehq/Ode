# Pop lyrics

Pop lyrics aim for instant clarity: a listener should understand the chorus on first hearing and be singing it by the second. Everything below serves that aim. The general craft (section roles, show-don't-tell, imagery, options, checks) is in the lyrics skill; this file adds only what is specific to pop.

## Get to the hook quickly

- Intros are short, and the first chorus usually arrives within about the first minute. Keep verse 1 lean so the song doesn't make the listener wait.
- Verse 2 is often shorter than verse 1 in modern pop (for example half the length, going straight into the pre-chorus). Ask before shortening the musician's structure; suggest it when verse 2 is struggling for new material.
- Every verse line should earn its place: one clear detail per line is plenty.

## The chorus and its title

- The title is usually in the first line of the chorus, the last line, or both, and it is often repeated several times per chorus. In pop, repeating the title is the hook working, not a lack of ideas.
- The title should be the most singable phrase in the song: short, rhythmic, with an open vowel on the word that's held.
- The chorus should be simpler and more general than the verses: everyday words, short lines, no long sentences that run across several lines.
- Common chorus shapes (described by what the lines do):
  - title, answer line, title, answer line;
  - three setup lines and the title last, with the title line left unrhymed so it stands out;
  - the title as a short chant, with one line that changes;
  - lines that start the same way, building to the title.
- Change one word or one line in the last chorus to deepen the meaning, so the final repeat lands harder than the first.

**Example (original), three setup lines and the title last:**

> *Tell them I'm away* (5)
> *Tell them I'm a ghost today* (7)
> *Tell them try again in May* (7)
> *I'm out of office* (5)

The lines that start the same way build momentum, "away / today / May" are perfect rhymes, and the title line breaks the pattern so it sticks. Syllable counts are in brackets.

## Pre-chorus

- Shorter lines and a sense of rising pressure, often matched by the production building.
- Move from verse detail toward feeling: a question, a "when" or "if" clause, a realisation that the chorus answers.
- Keep the title out of it so the chorus gets the payoff.
- Leave it unresolved (an odd number of lines, or an unrhymed last line) so it leans into the chorus.

## Post-chorus

- A short repeated hook after the chorus: one to four words, often a fragment of the title, a single word, or a vowel sound.
- Rhythmic and easy to sing. It often works with the production (a vocal chop, a synth line), so write it with the track playing.
- Repeating the same word is normal here.

## Words and lines

- Everyday, short words; contractions; normal word order. Avoid obscure references and words most listeners would need to look up.
- Aim for universal and specific at once: a situation any listener can step into, with one or two details that make it unmistakably this song.
- "You" addressed directly is common because it lets listeners put themselves in the song. Don't give "you" a gender unless the musician has.
- Present tense is common in pop because it feels immediate.
- A twist on an everyday phrase makes a strong pop title.

## Singing at tempo

- At dance tempos, fast syllables need simple consonants. Save consonant clusters for slower moments.
- Leave rests. A chorus packed wall to wall with syllables has no room for the hook to breathe.
- Put held notes on open vowels, especially the title's key word (see the prosody skill).

## Writing to a track or topline

Pop toplines often come before the words, sung with placeholder syllables or half-words. When the musician gives you a topline:

- Take the syllable count and stress pattern of each line from the melody; the melody is fixed unless they say otherwise.
- Keep the placeholder's vowel on long notes. If they sang "oh" on the long note, look for a word with that vowel there; it's the sound they fell for.
- Keep the placeholder's rhythm, including where phrases start before or after the beat.
- If a placeholder phrase already sounds like real words, it may be the title. Point it out.

## Pop checks

On top of the lyrics skill's checks:
- The chorus is understandable on first listen.
- The title is in the first or last chorus line and repeats.
- Any post-chorus hook is short and easy to sing.
- Every chorus line is singable at the song's tempo, with rests where the singer needs them.
- Verse 2 brings something new rather than repeating verse 1 in different words.
