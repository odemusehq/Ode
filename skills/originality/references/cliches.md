# Clichés: what's underneath and where to go instead

Use these lists to recognise stock language quickly and to find the real feeling it is standing in for. None of these phrases is banned: a musician may keep one on purpose, and an everyday phrase given a twist can make a great title. Flag them, explain, and offer fresher options.

## 1. Stock phrases

| Stock phrase | The feeling underneath | Where to look instead |
|---|---|---|
| heart of stone, cold heart | numbness, refusing to feel | a specific act of refusal: an unanswered call, a gift left wrapped |
| broken heart, heart in pieces | grief after a loss | what the singer can no longer do, or does differently now |
| tears like rain, tears falling down | crying | where, when, and who might see; what the singer does to hide it |
| set me free, break these chains | wanting release | the specific thing they're stuck in, and the first step out |
| lost without you | dependence | an everyday task they can't manage alone |
| end of the road | the end of a relationship | the last ordinary moment before it ended |
| light in the darkness, you're my light | someone who helps | what that person actually does, on an actual day |
| burning bridges | ruining a relationship | the thing that was said or done |
| time stood still | an intense moment | one sensory detail of that moment |
| on top of the world | elation | what joy makes the body do |
| walls around my heart | guardedness | a habit of avoidance |
| fire in my soul, burning desire | passion | a physical detail: hands, breath, distance |
| chasing dreams | ambition | what is being given up for it |
| written in the stars, meant to be | fate | the coincidence that brought them together |
| dancing in the rain | carefree joy | a specific, slightly ridiculous moment of letting go |
| one more chance | regret | the concrete thing they would do differently |
| scars that run deep | old hurt | an everyday reminder of it |

## 2. Stock images

When the image is the first one anyone would think of, it carries no surprise:

- rain or storms for sadness and trouble;
- sunshine for happiness;
- fire and flames for passion;
- chains for being trapped; wings and flying for freedom;
- the open road for escape or life's journey;
- the ocean for depth of love; stars for destiny;
- ghosts for memories; shadows for fear; mirrors for self-examination.

Use one only with a twist (see the wipers example in SKILL.md), or replace it with an image from the song's own world: the singer's street, job, kitchen, commute, weather, town.

## 3. Familiar situations told in familiar words

The rainy breakup, the small-town escape, the one that got away, eyes meeting across a crowded room, the late-night drive, the last dance. These situations aren't the problem; plenty of great songs live in them. The problem is telling them with the stock phrases and images above. Keep the situation, and find the details only this song could have.

## 4. Words that make lyrics sound generic

Words that turn up constantly in generic or machine-written lyrics and rarely in the way people talk. Avoid them unless the musician uses them in their own writing:

echoes, whispers, shadows, shattered, fragments, embers, ashes, neon, tapestry, symphony, serenade, labyrinth, kaleidoscope, ethereal, celestial, velvet, crimson, silhouette, cascade, ignite, soar, "in the void", "dance with the stars".

The fix is almost always the plainer, more specific word.

## 5. Over-used rhyme pairs

The list, with ways to find fresher rhymes, is in the rhymes skill. In short: fire / desire, heart / apart, love / above, pain / rain, night / light, eyes / lies, soul / control, cry / goodbye, forever / together, baby / maybe, true / you. Flag them, especially in a chorus, where they are heard again and again.
