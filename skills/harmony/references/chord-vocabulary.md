# Chord vocabulary

Tables for the harmony skill. Examples are in C major and A minor; transpose as needed. Progressions are common property; what makes a song original is how its melody, rhythm and words use them.

## 1. Diatonic seventh chords

| Degree | C major | Function | A natural minor | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cmaj7 | tonic | Am7 | tonic |
| 2 | Dm7 | subdominant | Bm7♭5 | subdominant |
| 3 | Em7 | tonic (weak) | Cmaj7 | tonic (relative major) |
| 4 | Fmaj7 | subdominant | Dm7 | subdominant |
| 5 | G7 | dominant | Em7 (or E7 from harmonic minor) | dominant |
| 6 | Am7 | tonic / subdominant | Fmaj7 | subdominant |
| 7 | Bm7♭5 | dominant | G7 (or G♯dim7 from harmonic minor) | dominant / modal |

## 2. Modes and their characteristic chords

Each mode on C, with the chord that gives it away. Use the characteristic chord to suggest the mode without abandoning the key's feel.

| Mode | Notes on C | Character | Characteristic move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ionian (major) | C D E F G A B | plain major | G7–C |
| Dorian | C D E♭ F G A B♭ | minor with a raised 6th; lifted, soulful | Cm–F (major IV) |
| Phrygian | C D♭ E♭ F G A♭ B♭ | minor with a ♭2; dark, tense | Cm–D♭ |
| Lydian | C D E F♯ G A B | major with a ♯4; dreamy, floating | C–D/C (major II) |
| Mixolydian | C D E F G A B♭ | major with a ♭7; earthy, rock and folk | C–B♭–F–C |
| Aeolian (natural minor) | C D E♭ F G A♭ B♭ | minor; plain or dramatic | Cm–A♭–B♭ |

Locrian (♭2 and ♭5) rarely works as a home key.

## 3. Borrowed chords

In C major:

| Chord | Borrowed from | Changed note | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fm, Fm6 | C minor | A♭ | bittersweet turn; Fm–C plagal ending |
| Dm7♭5 | C minor | A♭ | softer predominant before G7 |
| A♭, A♭maj7 | C minor | A♭, E♭ | lift or drama; deceptive G–A♭ |
| B♭ | C minor or C Mixolydian | B♭ | bright lift into C; rock cadence B♭–C |
| E♭ | C minor | E♭, B♭ | bold colour, often next to A♭ or B♭ |
| Gm | C minor or Mixolydian | B♭ | soft, modal dominant |
| D (major II) | C Lydian | F♯ | dreamy lift, C–D/C |
| D♭ | C Phrygian (the Neapolitan chord) | D♭, A♭ | dark approach to C or G |

In A minor:

| Chord | Borrowed from | Changed note | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| D (major IV) | A Dorian or A major | F♯ | lifted minor, Am–D vamp |
| A (major I) | A major | C♯ | a bright final chord |
| E, E7 | A harmonic minor | G♯ | strong cadence into Am |
| F♯m7♭5 | A Dorian | F♯ | predominant with a Dorian colour |

## 4. Secondary dominants and leading-tone chords

In C major:

| Target | Secondary dominant | Its leading tone | Secondary leading-tone chord |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dm (ii) | A7 | C♯ | C♯dim7 |
| Em (iii) | B7 | D♯ | D♯dim7 |
| F (IV) | C7 | E (with the added B♭ as 7th) | Edim7 |
| G (V) | D7 | F♯ | F♯dim7 |
| Am (vi) | E7 | G♯ | G♯dim7 |

In A minor:

| Target | Secondary dominant | Leading tone |
|---|---|---|
| Dm (iv) | A7 | C♯ |
| F (♭VI) | C7 | E (B♭ added) |
| C (♭III) | G7 | B |
| G (♭VII) | D7 | F♯ |
| E (V) | B7 | D♯ |

A secondary ii–V puts the target's own ii before its dominant: Em7–A7–Dm7, Am7–D7–G.

## 5. Common progressions and their feel

| Progression (C major unless noted) | Numerals | Feel and use |
|---|---|---|
| C – G – Am – F | I–V–vi–IV | open, anthemic loop |
| Am – F – C – G | vi–IV–I–V | the same chords starting darker; rising |
| C – Am – F – G | I–vi–IV–V | classic, nostalgic |
| C – F – Am – G | I–IV–vi–V | warm and moving |
| F – G – Am | IV–V–vi | lifting, unresolved |
| F – C – G – Am | IV–I–V–vi | starts away from home; floating |
| Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 | ii–V–I | smooth; jazz, soul |
| C – B♭ – F – C | I–♭VII–IV–I | Mixolydian; rock and folk |
| C – A♭ – B♭ – C | I–♭VI–♭VII–I | heroic lift |
| C – E7 – Am – F | I–V/vi–vi–IV | a dramatic pull into Am |
| Am – G – F – E | i–♭VII–♭VI–V in A minor (the Andalusian cadence) | descending, dramatic |
| Am – F – C – G (in A minor) | i–♭VI–♭III–♭VII | the same chords as vi–IV–I–V, heard in minor |
| C – Cmaj7 – C7 – F | a line cliché (C–B–B♭–A inside the chord) | movement over one chord |
| Am – Am(maj7) – Am7 – Am6 | minor line cliché (A–G♯–G–F♯) | tension, drama |
| C7 F7 C7 C7 / F7 F7 C7 C7 / G7 F7 C7 G7 | 12-bar blues | blues, rock'n'roll |

## 6. Passing and chromatic chords

- **Diminished passing chords** fill the gap between chords a whole step apart: `C – C♯dim7 – Dm7`, `Dm7 – D♯dim7 – C/E`.
- **Chromatic approach chords**: any chord a half step above or below the target, briefly (`D♭7 – C`, `B7 – C` sounds tense).
- **Augmented chords** for motion: `C – Caug – Am` or `C – Caug – F` (the raised fifth moves on by step).
- **Minor iv6 in a turnaround**: `C – Fm6 – C`.
- **Line clichés**: one chromatic line moving inside a held chord (section 5).

## 7. Modulation routes

| From → to | Route | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Up a half step (C → D♭) | the new key's V | `G7 – A♭7 – D♭` |
| Up a whole step (C → D) | the new key's ii–V | `C – Em7 A7 – D` |
| To the relative minor (C → Am) | V of the new tonic | `C – E7 – Am` |
| To the dominant (C → G) | pivot vi = ii | `C – Am – D7 – G` |
| To the subdominant (C → F) | I becomes V7 | `C – C7 – F` |
| To ♭VI (C → A♭) | common tone C | `C – E♭ – A♭` or directly at a section boundary |
| To III (C → E) | common tone E | `C – B7 – E` |
| Back home | a pivot, or restate the original key at a new section | — |

Before suggesting any upward key change, check the melody's highest notes against the singer's range.

## 8. Spelling

- Build chords in stacked thirds on alternating letters: C–E–G–B♭ is C7; C–E–G–A♯ is a misspelling.
- Use flats in flat keys and sharps in sharp keys, matching the key signature.
- Secondary dominants take the accidental that leads to their target: D7 is D–F♯–A–C; E7 is E–G♯–B–D.
- Diminished sevenths are spelled from their root: C♯dim7 is C♯–E–G–B♭.
