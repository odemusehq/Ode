# Pop harmony

Only what is specific to pop. The loop (read, decide, change in place, verify, let them hear it, report in one line), function, cadences, borrowing, voicing and the general checks are in the harmony skill.

## Loops are normal

Many pop songs run two to four chords through the whole song. The craft lies in what changes around the loop. Common loops (common property):

| Loop (C major) | Numerals | Character |
|---|---|---|
| C – G – Am – F | I–V–vi–IV | open, anthemic |
| Am – F – C – G | vi–IV–I–V | the same chords starting darker |
| C – Am – F – G | I–vi–IV–V | classic |
| F – C – G – Am | IV–I–V–vi | starts away from home, floats |
| Am – F – C – G in A minor | i–♭VI–♭III–♭VII | minor-key pop and dance |
| C – F, or Am – F | two-chord vamps | groove and topline carry the song |

The same four chords feel different depending on where the loop starts and which chord the melody treats as home.

## Making sections contrast over the same loop

When "every section sounds the same", change one or two of these per section boundary, in the section's own chord and bass clips:

1. **Start the loop on another chord** in the chorus: verse `Am – F – C – G`, chorus `F – C – G – Am`.
2. **Change the harmonic rhythm**: two bars per chord in the verse, one in the chorus.
3. **Change the bass**: hold a tonic pedal under the verse (`Am – F/A – C/A – G/A`) and release it in the chorus; or use inversions for a stepwise line (`C – G/B – Am – F`).
4. **Change the voicing and register**: low, close voicings in the verse; wide, high ones in the chorus.
5. **Take the chords out**: bass and vocal only in the verse, chords entering at the pre-chorus (the arrangement skill mutes or removes the part; ask before deleting it).
6. **Add one new chord in one place**: a borrowed iv or ♭VII in the last bar before the chorus.

## Pre-chorus

- Move to subdominant chords (IV, ii) and end open, on V, IV or a sus chord, so the chorus downbeat is the release.
- Rising bass lines add push: `| Dm7 | Em7 | Fmaj7 | Gsus4 G |` climbs by step and ends on the dominant.
- A bar of IV/V (F/G) right before the chorus is a soft dominant that pop uses constantly.

## Chorus

- Starting on I sounds like arrival; starting on IV lifts without fully landing; starting on vi gives a bittersweet weight.
- If the chorus repeats, end its first pass on V or IV so it turns around; close on I only at the very end of the last chorus, if at all.

## Pop colours

- **add9 and sus2** (Cadd9, Csus2): open and modern. **sus4 resolving to the 3rd**: a small push inside one chord.
- **maj7** for softness, more common in pop R&B and ballads.
- **Slash chords**: IV/V (F/G) as a soft dominant; I/3 (C/E) and V/7 (G/B) for smooth bass lines.
- **Borrowed ♭VII** (B♭ in C) for a bright lift; **♭VI–♭VII–I** (A♭–B♭–C) as a climactic cadence; **iv** (Fm in C) for a bittersweet turn in the last line of a chorus or in a bridge.
- **III7 to vi** (E7–Am in C): a secondary dominant with a dramatic pull.

## Modal pop

- **Mixolydian** (`C – B♭ – F – C`): earthy, rock-pop.
- **Dorian** (`Am – D`, a minor tonic with a major IV): a lifted, soulful minor.
- **Aeolian** loops (`Am – F – C – G` heard in A minor): darker dance-pop and ballads.
- **Lydian** (`C – D/C`): dreamy, floating.

## Bridge

- Take the song somewhere new: start on a chord still unused (often IV, vi or ♭VI), slow down or speed up the harmonic rhythm, or move to the relative minor or major.
- Return through V, or a held IV/V, into the last chorus so the chorus downbeat feels like coming home.

## Final-chorus key change

- Up a half or whole step at the chorus downbeat, prepared by the new key's V or ii–V, or unprepared for a punch.
- It moves every part, so it is a question unless the musician asked for it, and the melody must pass the range check in the new key.
- Other ways to lift without a key change: higher voicings, the full band entering, chords dropping out for the first half then returning, a countermelody or an extra vocal harmony.

## Minimal harmony

Some pop runs on two chords or one. Then rhythm, bass movement, sound and the topline carry the interest; resist adding chords only for variety.

## Mood

Avoid the "minor means sad" shortcut. Many heartbroken pop songs sit on bright major loops and many uplifting ones are in minor. Choose chords by colour and by what the lyric is doing.

## Worked example

The musician selects the verse keys and types "every section sounds the same": the loop `| C | G | Am | F |` runs under verse and chorus alike. Ode keeps the chorus as it is and changes the verse: a tonic pedal in the bass (`C – G/C – Am/C – F/C`) and low, close voicings in the keys, edited in the verse's own bass and keys clips; the lead sheet's verse chart becomes the slash chords. The chorus's root-position bass now sounds like a release.

Result line: "Verse now over a C pedal with low, close voicings, chorus unchanged · melody fits every chord, verse and chorus differ in bass and register."

Other takes for Again: start the chorus on IV (`| F | C/E | Dm7 | Gsus4 G |`) so it lifts and keeps moving; or a Dorian-coloured verse in A minor, `| Am7 | D9 | Am7 | D9 |` — the D chord's F♯ needs a verse melody free of F natural, and for an artist who always stays in major keys it is outside the usual style, so the result line says so.

## Checks for pop

Run these with the harmony skill's checks.

| Check | Correct when |
|---|---|
| Contrast | neighbouring sections differ beyond the lyric, in start chord, bass, harmonic rhythm, voicing or density |
| Pre-chorus | it ends open, away from I, so the chorus downbeat is the release |
| Turnaround | a repeating chorus ends its first pass on V or IV |
| Key change | after one, the melody passes the range check in the new key |

## Result lines

- "Pre-chorus now climbs Dm7 – Em7 – Fmaj7 – Gsus4 G under a rising bass · melody fits, ends on V into the chorus."
- "Last chorus line borrows Fm before the final C · melody there clear of A natural, bass on F, chart updated."
- "Chorus loop start moved from C to F · same chords, melody fits, verse and chorus now differ in where the loop lands."
