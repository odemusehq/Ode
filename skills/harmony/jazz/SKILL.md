# Jazz harmony

Only what is specific to jazz. The loop (read, decide, change in place, verify, let them hear it, report in one line), function, cadences, borrowing, the general checks and the conventions (scientific pitch, chord symbols, lengths in beats) are in the harmony skill.

## Seventh chords are the default

The basic unit is the seventh chord: maj7, m7, 7, m7♭5 and dim7, with 6 and m6 as alternative tonic chords. Triads sound plain or deliberately bare in a jazz context.

## ii–V–I and guide tones

- Major: `Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7`. Minor: `Dm7♭5 – G7♭9 – Cm6` (or Cm(maj7), Cm7).
- Guide tones are each chord's 3rd and 7th. Through a ii–V–I the 7th of each chord falls by a half or whole step to the 3rd of the next (C → B, F → E), and the 3rd holds to become the next 7th: Dm7 (F, C) → G7 (F, B) → Cmaj7 (E, B). Build voicings around this line; it is what makes jazz changes sound connected.

## Turnarounds and ii–V variations

- Turnarounds back to I: `Cmaj7 – Am7 – Dm7 – G7` (I–vi–ii–V); `C – A7 – Dm7 – G7` (I–VI7–ii–V); `Em7 – A7 – Dm7 – G7` (iii–VI–ii–V); with tritone substitutions `Cmaj7 – E♭7 – Dm7 – D♭7`.
- Secondary ii–Vs: put any target chord's own ii–V before it — `Gm7 – C7 – Fmaj7`, `Bm7♭5 – E7 – Am7`.
- Backdoor ii–V: `Fm7 – B♭7 – Cmaj7` (iv7–♭VII7–I), a softer approach borrowed from the minor.

## Tritone substitution

- Replace a dominant with the dominant a tritone away; they share the 3rd and 7th (G7 has B and F; D♭7 has F and C♭, which sounds as B). `Dm7 – D♭7 – Cmaj7` gives a chromatic bass line.
- Over the substitute, the original dominant's natural 9th, 13th and 5th (A, E and D over G7) become altered tensions (♯5, ♯9, ♭9 of D♭7). That suits bebop-flavoured settings and sounds harsh in a gentle ballad: let the song's feel decide.

## Diminished chords

- Passing: `Cmaj7 – C♯dim7 – Dm7`, `Dm7 – D♯dim7 – Em7`.
- As a dominant: Bdim7 is G7♭9 without its root, and any of its inversions (Ddim7, Fdim7, A♭dim7) can stand in.

## Extensions and alterations

Let the melody choose. If the melody has A♭ over G7, the chord is G7♭9; if it has A, the chord is G9 or G13. Choose only alterations that agree with the melody.

| Chord | Usual tensions | Handle with care |
|---|---|---|
| maj7 (I, IV) | 9, ♯11, 13 (or 6) | natural 11, a half step above the 3rd; on IVmaj7 the ♯11 is already in the key |
| m7 as ii (Dorian) | 9, 11, 13 | — |
| m7 as vi (Aeolian) | 9, 11 | ♭13, a half step above the 5th |
| m7 as iii (Phrygian) | 11 | ♭9 and ♭13 |
| 7, unaltered | 9, 13, ♯11 | natural 11 against the 3rd (write it as a sus4 chord) |
| 7, altered | ♭9, ♯9, ♯11 (♭5), ♭13 (♯5) | natural 9 or 13 mixed with altered tensions in one voicing |
| m7♭5 | 11, ♭13; natural 9 if the melody has it | ♭9 |
| dim7 | the notes a whole step above each chord tone | — |

- A dominant resolving to a minor chord prefers ♭9 and ♭13, because both belong to the minor key.
- Resolve alterations smoothly: ♭9 falls to the 5th of the next chord (A♭ to G over C), ♭13 falls to the 9th or rises to the 3rd.

## Voicings

- **Shell**: root, 3rd and 7th (or root, 7th, 3rd). Clear, light, and leaves room for the melody; the standard left-hand and guitar voicing.
- **Rootless** (when a bass instrument plays the roots): A form 3–5–7–9, B form 7–9–3–5 (on dominant chords the 13th usually replaces the 5th). Alternating forms keeps the hand in one place through a ii–V–I: Dm9 `F3 A3 C4 E4` → G13 `F3 A3 B3 E4` → Cmaj9 `E3 G3 B3 D4`. Keep rootless voicings roughly between D3 and C5, where they sound clear.
- **Drop 2**: take a close four-note voicing and drop the second voice from the top by an octave. Cmaj7 `C4 E4 G4 B4` becomes `G3 C4 E4 B4`. A staple on guitar and piano.
- **Quartal**: stacked fourths, open and modal. Dm11 as `D3 G3 C4 F4` (root, 11, 7, 3).
- **Upper-structure triads** over a dominant: D/C7 (D, F♯, A over C, E, B♭) gives C13♯11; A♭/C7 (A♭, C, E♭) gives C7♯9♭13.

## Comping rhythm

When the request is about feel ("swing the keys", "more bossa"), change the chord part's rhythm in place along with the voicings:

- Swing comping is short and syncopated, often anticipating the beat by an eighth note (the "and" of 2 or 4). The Charleston rhythm — hits on beat 1 and the "and" of 2 — is a common starting point.
- Ballads sustain fuller voicings with fewer changes of rhythm.
- Bossa nova has a steady, syncopated chord pattern over a bass on beats 1 and 3 (root and fifth).
- Leave space when the melody is busy, and fill when it holds.

## Jazz blues

12-bar blues in F (common property):

```
| F7  | B♭7 | F7    | Cm7 F7 |
| B♭7 | Bdim7 | F7  | D7♭9   |
| Gm7 | C7  | F7 D7 | Gm7 C7 |
```

Minor blues in C:

```
| Cm7 | Fm7 | Cm7 | Cm7         |
| Fm7 | Fm7 | Cm7 | Cm7         |
| A♭7 | G7♭9 | Cm7 | Dm7♭5 G7♭9 |
```

## AABA bridges

In 32-bar AABA tunes the bridge (B) often moves to IV or to a key a third away and comes back through a ii–V to the last A. The song-form skill covers the form itself.

## Worked example: "jazzier"

The musician selects the keys under a melody in C major — E4 held through bar 1; F4 for two beats then D4 for two in bar 2; E4 then G4, two beats each, in bar 3; C4 held through bar 4 — over `| C | G7 | C | C |`, and types "jazzier".

Ode rewrites the keys in place as `| Cmaj7 | Dm7 G7 | Em7 A7 | Dm7 G7sus4 |` in rootless voicings between D3 and C5, moves the bass notes on each change to the new roots, and updates the chart. Checks: E is the 3rd of Cmaj7; F the 3rd of Dm7, D the 5th of G7; E the root of Em7, G the 7th of A7; C the 7th of Dm7 and the 4th of G7sus4; guide tones move by step. Bar 4 becomes a turnaround back to the top; if this is the end of the song, bar 4 holds Cmaj7 or C6.

Result line: "Keys now Cmaj7 – Dm7 G7 – Em7 A7 – Dm7 G7sus4 in rootless voicings, bass following · every melody note fits, guide tones move by step."

Other takes for Again: substitutions — `| Cmaj7 | Dm7 G7 | Em7 E♭7 | Dm7 D♭maj7 |`, where E♭7 replaces A7 (G is its 3rd) and D♭maj7 approaches C from a half step above (C is its major 7th), with the bass walking down E–E♭–D–D♭ into C; or, outside the usual style for an artist who keeps things diatonic, `| Cmaj7♯11 | Fm9 B♭13 | Em7 A7♭9 | Dm9 G7sus4 |` voiced in fourths — a Lydian colour, a backdoor ii–V (F is the root of Fm9, D the 3rd of B♭13) and an altered dominant into Dm9 (G is the 7th of A7♭9). Every take keeps the melody exactly as written.

## Checks for jazz

Run these with the harmony skill's checks.

| Check | Correct when |
|---|---|
| Guide tones | the 3rds and 7ths move by a half or whole step (or hold) from chord to chord |
| Tensions | every extension or alteration agrees with the melody note above it; each voicing keeps natural 9 and 13 apart from altered tensions |
| Substitutions | tritone substitutions and diminished chords make the bass line smoother |
| Register | rootless and shell voicings sit roughly between D3 and C5, out of the melody's way |

## Result lines

- "ii–V into bar 5 now Gm7 C7♭9 · melody's D♭ fits as the ♭9, guide tones move by step."
- "Comping changed to Charleston hits on 1 and the 'and' of 2, drop-2 voicings · melody fits, only the bass below D3."
- "Last bar now D♭7 into C · tritone substitution, the melody's B sounds as its 7th, bass falls by half step."
