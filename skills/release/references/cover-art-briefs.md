# Cover art briefs

How to write a brief the image model can turn into artwork that fits the song, how to handle "make it like…" requests, and the store rules that get artwork rejected.

## Finding the images in a song

- **Read the lyrics for pictures:** objects, places, weather, times of day, colours, actions. One strong, specific image beats a collage of everything in the song.
- **Read the music for mood:** a slow, sparse song suggests calm, open compositions; a dense, fast one suggests energy, contrast and movement. A warm, saturated sound suggests warm colours; a cold, clean one suggests cooler, cleaner images.
- **Read the title:** sometimes it is the image; sometimes the best cover shows something next to the title and leaves the literal illustration aside.
- **When the lyrics are abstract,** use the lead sheet's theme and story; the cover can carry what the words leave unsaid.
- Avoid genre shorthand (a sad song can be any colour and any weather). Choose from this song's own details.

## Brief template

```
Song: <title> — <one-line essence of what the song is about>
Key images from the lyrics: <two or three concrete images>
Mood: <three to five mood words>
Subject: <the one main thing in the picture>
Setting: <where and when>
Composition: <close-up or wide, centred or off-centre, how much empty space>
Colour palette: <two to four colours, warm or cool, saturated or muted>
Light: <soft or hard, time of day, light source>
Medium and style: <photograph, painting, illustration, collage, 3D render…; grain, texture>
Avoid: text (unless asked), logos and brands, real people's likeness, other artists' artwork, <anything the musician dislikes>
Text: leave it out (or the exact title and artist name, if the musician asks)
Format: square, 3000 × 3000 pixels, RGB; must read at thumbnail size
```

## Worked example: three briefs, three images

An original example song: "Lantern Season", about leaving a hometown at the end of summer, mid-tempo, acoustic guitar and soft synths, warm and slightly melancholic. Lyric images: a ferry at dusk, paper lanterns on a railing, a suitcase too full to close.

1. **Ferry at dusk.** Photograph from the back of a ferry, the town shrinking on the horizon, orange and lilac sky, lots of empty space above, soft film grain. Subject small and off-centre, so the distance is the feeling.
2. **Lanterns close up.** A tight shot of three paper lanterns on a rusted railing, one unlit, warm light against deep blue dusk, shallow focus. Simple, reads clearly as a thumbnail.
3. **Suitcase collage.** A cut-paper collage of a half-closed suitcase spilling a summer landscape (sea, lanterns, a ferry) in flat, bright colours on an off-white background. Playful, while the other two are wistful.

All three: without text, square, 3000 × 3000. One image per brief; the musician picks one from a single question that shows the three images. The collage departs from the artist's usual photographic covers, so the result line says so.

## Turning references into general qualities

When the musician says "like [artist]'s cover" or names a specific record, describe the qualities it has and build something new from them. Keep what is general; leave what identifies that artwork.

| Keep (general qualities) | Leave (what identifies the original) |
|---|---|
| Medium: photograph, painting, illustration | The specific subject, pose or scene |
| Colour approach: muted, black and white, one bold colour | The exact colour scheme combined with its layout |
| Composition: centred close-up, lots of empty space | The recognisable framing or crop |
| Mood: intimate, cold, playful, unsettling | Its typography, logo or lettering |
| Texture: film grain, paper, brush strokes | Any characters, objects or symbols associated with that artist |

Example: "like a minimalist photographic cover" becomes "candid photograph, muted colour, a single subject, a lot of empty space, natural light" — then the subject and scene come from this song's own lyrics.

If only recreating a specific cover would meet a request, make covers from its general qualities, and say in the result line that they are new artwork built from its qualities.

## If the musician wants text

- Use the exact title and artist name as in the metadata; platforms reject artwork whose text differs from it.
- Keep it legible at thumbnail size: large, high contrast, away from busy areas of the image.
- Keep it to the title and artist name alone, leaving out release dates, "out now", URLs and social handles.
- Image models often misspell text, and only a person looking at the image can check the lettering: give the exact title and artist in the brief, and say in the result line that the lettering needs a look.

## Refining a direction

- Change one thing at a time: the palette, the crop, the light, the subject's position. Several changes at once make it hard to tell what helped.
- Keep every version the musician has seen, so they can go back.
- When the musician picks one, check its brief against the store rules below.

## Rights

- Use an image model or service whose terms allow commercial use and leave the musician the rights to the artwork.
- Give it someone else's photograph or artwork as a base only when the musician has the rights to it.
- People who appear recognisably in a cover need to have given permission.
- Write in the clearance list how the artwork was made (generated, from what brief, or the photographer or artist who made it), for the credits and the clearance check.

## Common store rules

Distributors and stores commonly reject artwork that:
- has unequal sides, is smaller than they ask (commonly 3000 × 3000 pixels), or uses a colour mode outside RGB;
- is blurry, pixelated, stretched or has borders added to fit;
- has text that differs from the title or artist name in the metadata;
- includes logos, brands or trademarks owned by someone else;
- includes URLs, social handles, email addresses, prices or words like "new", "exclusive" or "out now";
- names or shows artists from outside the release, or real people without permission;
- includes sexually explicit or violent imagery, or hate symbols;
- reuses another release's artwork or misleads about who made the music.

Requirements vary by distributor; check theirs before upload.
