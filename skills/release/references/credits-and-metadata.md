# Credits, splits, codes and clearance

What to gather for credits and splits, what the industry codes are and who issues them, and the full clearance check before upload. Record what the musician and their collaborators tell you; never fill a gap with a guess, and mark missing items clearly.

## Credits to gather

| Who | What to collect | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Songwriters | Legal name, artist name if different, what they wrote (lyrics, music, both) | Anyone who contributed to melody, lyrics or harmony as agreed among the writers |
| Composition split | Each writer's share, totalling 100% | Agreed between the writers; Ode records it, never proposes who deserves what |
| Publishers | Each writer's publisher, or self-published | Needed for collecting songwriting royalties |
| Collecting society | Each writer's performing rights organisation or collecting society, and IPI number | Lets royalties reach the right person |
| Master owner | Who owns the recording (the artist, a label, shared) | Decides the ℗ line and who receives recording income |
| Producers | Names, roles (producer, co-producer, additional production), any royalty agreement | Producer agreements are separate from songwriting splits |
| Featured artists | Names as on their own artist profiles, and their approval of the release | Featured artists normally approve how they are credited |
| Performers | Vocals, instruments, programming | Often shown in platform credits |
| Engineers | Recording, mixing, mastering | Often shown in platform credits |
| Samples | Source, owner, clearance status | From the clearance list |
| Artwork | Who made it, or how it was generated | From the clearance list |

## Split sheet template

```
Song title:                         Date:
Recording or version:

Writer (legal name) | Artist name | Contribution (lyrics / music) | Share % | Publisher | Society | IPI | Signature
------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------- | ------- | --------- | ------- | --- | ---------
                    |             |                               |         |           |         |     |
                    |             |                               |         |           |         |     |
Total                                                               100%

Samples or interpolations used (source, owner, clearance status):

Notes:
```

Suggest that every writer signs before release; disagreements are far easier to settle before the song earns anything.

## Codes explained

| Code | Identifies | Format | Who issues it |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISRC | One recording | 12 characters: country code (2 letters), registrant code (3 characters), year (2 digits), designation (5 digits), often written CC-XXX-YY-NNNNN | The distributor, or the national ISRC agency for registrants |
| UPC or EAN | One release (single, EP, album) | UPC: 12 digits; EAN: 13 digits | Usually the distributor |
| ISWC | One composition | T-123.456.789-C (a T, nine digits and a check digit) | Assigned through publishers and collecting societies after registration |
| IPI | A songwriter or publisher | 9–11 digits | The writer's or publisher's collecting society |

**ISRC rules of thumb**
- Each distinct recording needs its own ISRC: the main version, the clean version, an instrumental, a remix, a radio edit, a live version.
- The same recording keeps the same ISRC when it appears on later releases (for example, a single that later appears on an album).
- Never reuse an ISRC for a different recording, and never make one up.

## Metadata style

- **Titles:** consistent capitalisation in the distributor's style; version information in parentheses or after a dash as the distributor asks ("(Acoustic)", "(Radio Edit)", "(Remix)"); nothing else in the title (no "Official Audio", "Prod. by", emojis or hashtags).
- **Artist names:** spelled exactly as on existing releases so the song lands on the right profile; featured artists in the field the distributor uses.
- **Genre:** chosen by the musician to describe the song, not inferred from who they are.
- **Explicit flag:** accurate. Explicit if any line needs it; a separately edited clean version marked clean.
- **Language:** the language of the lyrics, or instrumental.
- **℗ and © lines:** year and owner. ℗ covers the sound recording; © covers the composition or the artwork, depending on the distributor's definition.
- **Release date:** agreed with the musician; leave weeks of lead time where editorial pitching matters.

## Clearance list

Keep one entry for every element that wasn't played or programmed from scratch by the musician and their collaborators:

```
Element:        <e.g. vocal chop in chorus, crackle texture, kick one-shot, cover art>
Source:         <source recording and its owner / sample library and licence / generated, with the description used>
Rights:         <owned by the musician / licensed under … / generated by a service whose terms allow commercial use and leave the musician the rights / needs clearance>
Status:         <cleared / to clear / to replace>
Credit needed:  <yes, and how / no>
```

## Full clearance check

Before upload, go through every item and confirm with the musician:

- [ ] Every sample from someone else's recording is cleared for both the recording and the composition, or has been replaced.
- [ ] Every sound isolated from a recording comes from a recording the musician owns or has cleared.
- [ ] Every library sample or loop is used within its licence.
- [ ] Every generated element (sounds, artwork, any generated vocal kept in the final song) comes from a model or service whose terms allow commercial use and leave the musician the rights.
- [ ] People recognisable on the artwork have given permission.
- [ ] Featured artists and collaborators have approved the release and their credits.
- [ ] Splits are agreed, ideally on a signed split sheet.
- [ ] The musician knows how to answer the distributor's questions about samples and AI tools, from the clearance list.

Anything unchecked is a risk to the release; say plainly what it is and what would resolve it.

## The release folder

Keep everything for the release together in the project's release folder:
- master files for each version (main, clean, instrumental), named with title, version and format;
- the final artwork, plus every version the musician saw;
- the lyric sheet (and the clean version's, if different);
- credits, split information and metadata;
- the clearance list.
