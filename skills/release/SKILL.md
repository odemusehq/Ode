# Release

Release preparation turns a finished song into what a distributor and the listening platforms need: artwork, lyrics, credits and metadata, with every element cleared so the musician keeps the right to publish their song. Ode makes these as files in the project's release folder (Ode/release); it leaves the Set as it is, and the musician uploads through their own distributor.

Distributors and platforms each have their own requirements, and they change from time to time; the guidance below covers what is commonly asked.

## The loop

1. **Read.** The lead sheet: title, what the song is about (theme, story, mood, hook), credits, what is locked, and the notes on where samples and generated sounds came from; and the lyrics, stanza by stanza under their labels. The Set, from the project map: every element from outside the song's own playing and programming — each audio clip's file, each Simpler and Drum Rack sample, anything isolated or generated. What Ode knows about the artist (artist name, past covers, dislikes) and the spelling setting.
2. **Decide** three or four cover directions from this song's own images and mood, what the lyric sheet holds, what the credits can say from known facts, and the clearance status of every element.
3. **Make the files**; the Set stays exactly as it is:
   - **Cover art:** one image per brief, three or four in all. Then ask once which to use: one line whose answers are the covers ("Cover 1" … "Cover 4"), shown as their images; the pick becomes the release cover. Every version stays in the release folder.
   - **The lyric sheet,** from the lyrics, with their labels as the section labels.
   - **The credits,** from the lead sheet and what the musician has said. A missing fact that two to four answers cover (who wrote the lyrics, when only the musician and a named collaborator could have) is asked in one question; every other unknown fact is marked missing and named in the result line.
   - **The clearance list,** in the lead sheet's notes: every sampled, isolated or generated element with its source, its rights and its status.
4. **Verify** with the checks below, fixing what Ode can fix; what only the musician can resolve goes in the result line, worst first.
5. **Let them see it.** The musician sees the covers in the question that picks one, and the files are in the release folder; this step is visual only.
6. **Report in one line:** what was made, then " · " and what was checked or still blocks release. For example:
   - "Release files ready: cover 2 as you picked, lyric sheet, credits missing legal names, splits and publishers · vocal chop's source is unknown: clear it or replace it before upload."
   - "Cover 6 is the cover now: warmer, with the title in large type · the brief spells the title as in the metadata; the lettering needs a look."
   - "Lyric sheet updated from the lyrics: verse 2 rewritten, repeats written out · UK spelling, every line clean."

**Again** on cover art makes three or four new images from different briefs and asks again which to use; the earlier ones stay in the folder.

**Style context.** A requested new visual direction needs no warning about past covers. Mention an unrequested departure only when that comparison helps the musician choose.

## Checks

| Check | Correct when |
|---|---|
| Cover briefs | Each brief comes from this song (its title, story, lyric images, mood); asks for a square 3000 × 3000 RGB image; has text only when the musician asked, and any text spells the title and artist exactly as in the metadata; keeps out everything stores reject (logos, brands, other artists' names or likeness, URLs, "out now"); describes one clear subject that reads at thumbnail size |
| Lyric sheet | Matches the lyrics line for line, with their labels as section labels; every repeat written out in full as sung; spelling as the spelling setting says; the title identical to the lead sheet's |
| Originality | Zero lyric lines flagged as likely quotations; clichés are only a note |
| Credits | Every name and role comes from the lead sheet or the musician; splits, when given, add up to 100%; every entry is a fact someone gave |
| Clearance | Every element in the Set is on the clearance list with a known source. Anything taken from someone else's recording that is still uncleared, and anything whose source is unknown, is flagged |
| Explicit | Any explicit line means the release is flagged explicit, and a clean version needs its own lyric sheet |

When a recording of the final vocal exists, compare a transcription of it with the lyrics; lines sung differently are named in the result line, because the lyric sheet must match the recording and only the musician knows which version is right.

## Questions

Ask for facts only with buttons, and take every fact from its source: credits, splits, codes and dates come from the musician or their distributor. Ask at most two questions, each one line with buttons: which cover to use, with the covers as the answers shown as images; and one missing credit, when two to four answers cover it. Every other gap is marked missing in the credits and named in the result line. The usual defaults (a cover without text, the spelling setting) go ahead without a question.

## Rights and clearance

Ode is for songs the musician will publish commercially, so every element of the release has to be theirs to publish.

- **Generated material** (cover art, generated sounds, a demo vocal kept in the final song) is usable only when it comes from models or services whose terms allow commercial use and leave the musician the rights to the results.
- **Sounds isolated from recordings** are cleared only when the musician owns the source recording or has cleared it. A sound taken from someone else's record needs clearance, usually for both the recording and the composition, or replacing before release.
- **Library samples and loops** come with a licence; most sample packs, and the sounds that come with Live, allow use inside a song.
- **A sample whose source is unknown** is treated as uncleared until the musician says where it came from.
- **If the distributor asks whether AI tools were used,** the musician answers under its terms; the clearance list shows which parts were generated.

Uncleared samples are a common reason releases are taken down after they go out, so flag them plainly and early.

## Cover art

The image model creates artwork from a brief, so the brief decides the result. A good brief comes from the song itself; a generic idea of the genre stays out.

- **Start from the song:** its theme and story, the strongest images in the lyrics, the title, the mood and the feel of the music (tempo, density, warmth). When the lyrics are abstract, the lead sheet's theme and story carry the cover.
- **Text only when the musician asks.** When they do, use the exact title and artist name as in the metadata; platforms reject artwork whose text differs from it.
- **Format:** square, 3000 × 3000 pixels, RGB, JPG or PNG, the size most distributors ask for.
- **Works small:** most listeners see the cover as a thumbnail. One clear subject and strong contrast read at that size; fine detail gets lost.
- **Make only original artwork.** "Like [artist]'s cover" becomes general qualities (photographic or painted, colour or black and white, close-up or wide, minimal or busy, candid or staged) and something new built from them.
- **Avoid what stores reject:** logos and brands, other artists' names or likeness, real people without permission, URLs, social handles, prices or "new single" text, and imagery the platforms forbid.
- **Vary the three or four directions** in one clear way each (subject, medium, colour, framing), so picking one is easy.

**Brief fields:** song (title, one-line essence, key lyric images, mood words), subject, setting, composition, colour palette, light, medium and style, texture, what to avoid, text (only when asked) and format. A template, a worked example and the store rules are in `references/cover-art-briefs.md`.

## Lyric sheet

- **The words as sung on the final recording,** with section labels (Verse 1, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Outro) on the printable sheet; lyric services often want plain lines without labels, so follow the distributor's guidance.
- **Repeats written out in full,** line by line as sung; shorthand such as "Chorus x2" stays off the sheet.
- **Consistent spelling** in UK or US English as the spelling setting says, a capital at the start of each line, light and consistent punctuation.
- **Backing lines and ad-libs** in parentheses only when they are clearly part of the song.
- **The title** matches the metadata exactly.

## Credits and splits

Gather this information; the decisions in it belong to the musician and their collaborators.

- **Songwriters:** legal names (and artist names if different), what each wrote (lyrics, music or both), and the agreed split of the composition, adding up to 100%. A split sheet signed by every writer before release avoids disputes later.
- **Publishing:** each writer's publisher (or self-published), collecting society, and IPI number if they have one.
- **The recording:** who owns the master, the producers and any producer agreement, featured artists, performers, and the recording, mixing and mastering engineers.
- **Samples and generated elements:** from the clearance list.

Credits name people and companies with their roles; how to mention tools and AI assistance is the musician's decision within the distributor's terms. Splits, contracts and sample clearance are agreements between people: record what the musician says, point out what is missing or risky, and leave legal advice to professionals. Field-by-field details and a split sheet template are in `references/credits-and-metadata.md`.

## Metadata

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Title | Exact spelling and capitalisation; version information in the distributor's style, e.g. "(Acoustic)", "(Radio Edit)" |
| Primary artist | Exactly as on the artist's existing releases, so the song lands on the right artist profile |
| Featured artists | In the field the distributor uses for them, spelled as on their own profiles |
| Songwriters, producers | From the credits |
| Genre and subgenre | Only as the musician chooses; who they are stays out of it |
| Language | Language of the lyrics, or instrumental |
| Explicit flag | Explicit if any line needs it; a clean version flagged clean |
| ℗ line | Year and owner of the sound recording |
| © line | Year and owner of the composition or artwork, as the distributor defines it |
| ISRC | One per recording, from the distributor or the national ISRC agency; a new code for each different version; the same recording keeps its code on later releases |
| UPC or EAN | One per release (single, EP, album), usually from the distributor |
| ISWC | Identifies the composition; assigned through a publisher or collecting society; optional at upload |
| Release date, label | The label's name, or the artist's own label name if self-released |

## Files before upload

What the distributor will check, and Ode checks first where it can:
- **Audio:** the approved master of each version, in the format the distributor asks (commonly WAV, 16 or 24-bit, 44.1 kHz or higher), with loudness and true peak measured as intended, a clean start and end, and a clean version with every explicit word removed and its own ISRC.
- **Artwork:** 3000 × 3000, square, RGB, JPG or PNG, within the distributor's file-size limit.
- **Everything else:** title, artist names and spellings identical across artwork, lyric sheet, credits and metadata; credits complete and splits agreed; every element in the song cleared; copies of everything in the project's release folder.
- Uploading well before the release date helps: editorial pitching on many services needs the release in their system weeks ahead.
