# House drums

Only what is specific to house. The loop (read, decide, change in place, verify, let them hear it, report in one line), grid notation, velocities, the note map and the general checks are in the drums skill.

## Tempo and phrasing

- Usually around 118–128 BPM in 4/4: deep house towards the lower end, tech house around 124–128.
- Eight-, 16- and 32-bar phrases make conventional DJ transitions easier. Off-boundary changes and irregular phrasing remain available when the musician wants them.

## The core groove

```
        1 e + a 2 e + a 3 e + a 4 e + a
Kick    X . . . X . . . X . . . X . . .
Clap    . . . . X . . . . . . . X . . .
Hat op  . . o . . . o . . . o . . . o .
Shaker  x g x g x g x g x g x g x g x g
```

- **Kick** on every beat, steady velocity, its tail short enough to leave room for the bass.
- **Clap or snare** on 2 and 4; a clap layered with a snare, or two claps slightly apart, for width.
- **Open hat** on every off-beat — the lift that makes house move.
- **Shaker or closed hats** in sixteenths with a strong accent pattern; this is where the swing lives.

## Swing and percussion

- Swing the sixteenths gently, around 54–58%, on hats, shakers and percussion; leave the kick and clap straight.
- Percussion — congas, bongos, rimshots, toms, cowbell — in syncopated one- or two-bar patterns that sit between the kicks, varied or swapped every 8 or 16 bars.
- Deep house leans on softer kicks, more swing and more percussion; tech house on a punchy kick, rolling percussion and fewer, tighter sounds; Afro house on layered, interlocking percussion.

## Variations

- Drop the kick for the last beat or bar of a phrase, so the next downbeat lands harder.
- An extra kick on the "a" of 4, or a clap on the "a" of 4, at phrase ends.
- A ride on the off-beats or in quarters for peak sections.
- A longer open-hat decay lifts a peak section and a shorter one tightens a groove: set it on the hat's pad itself, in place. A gradual sweep across a phrase needs automation, which only the musician can draw; when a request needs one, the result line says so.

## Arrangement for the dance floor

- **Intro and outro**: 16–32 bars of kick, hats and percussion with few other elements, so a DJ can mix in and out. Elements come in every 8 or 16 bars.
- **Breakdown**: the kick (and often the bass) drops out for 8–32 bars; the chords, vocal or a pad carry it.
- **Build**: 4–8 bars of rising tension — a clap or snare roll, risers, a filter opening — into the drop.
- **Drop**: the kick returns on the downbeat, often with a crash or an impact, and the full groove.

A four-bar clap build into a drop, velocity rising from about 60 to 120:

```
Bar 1 (quarters)    X . . . X . . . X . . . X . . .
Bar 2 (eighths)     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Bar 3 (sixteenths)  x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x x
Bar 4 (sixteenths)  x x x x x x x x x x x x . . . .
```

The silent last beat before the drop makes the returning kick hit harder. The build is written into the last four bars of the breakdown's own drum clip; the crash and kick go on the first beat of the drop's clip.

## Kick and bass

- In many house styles the bass plays the off-beats between the kicks: the kick owns the beat and the bass answers it. The bass skill writes the bass rhythm to match.
- Ducking the bass and pads with the kick (sidechain compression) gives the familiar pumping; that is a mixing choice, covered by the mixing skill.

## Checks for house

Use these as references for a conventional house brief alongside the drums skill; they do not override the chosen phrasing, swing or kit.

| Check | What to review |
|---|---|
| Four on the floor | a kick on every beat, or a deliberate drop-out at a phrase end or in a breakdown |
| Phrases | compare pattern changes with the intended DJ phrasing, retaining deliberate off-boundary changes |
| Swing | inspect the requested feel; 54–58% hats with straight kick/clap is one starting point |
| DJ intro and outro | when requested, review mixing room against the DJ brief; 16–32 sparse bars is a conventional option |
| Kick and bass | where the bass plays the off-beats, every bass note sits between the kicks |
