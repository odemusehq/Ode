# Frequency guide

Where sounds live and what common problem words usually point to. These ranges tell you where to start looking; every recording and every sound is different, so find the real frequency on the actual sound by listening and by its spectrum.

## The bands

| Band | Range | Gives | Too much sounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sub | 20–60 Hz | Weight you feel more than hear; many small speakers can't reproduce it | Rumbling, eats headroom |
| Bass | 60–250 Hz | Fullness, warmth, punch | Boomy, heavy |
| Low mids | 250–500 Hz | Body | Muddy, cloudy |
| Mids | 500 Hz–2 kHz | Character, tone | Boxy (around 400–800 Hz), honky or nasal (around 800 Hz–1.5 kHz) |
| Upper mids | 2–5 kHz | Presence, clarity, attack; the ear is most sensitive here | Harsh, hard, tiring |
| Highs | 5–10 kHz | Brightness, definition | Sibilant (around 5–9 kHz), splashy |
| Air | 10–20 kHz | Openness, sparkle | Hissy, brittle |

## Problem words

| Musician says | Usually look at | Common moves |
|---|---|---|
| Muddy, cloudy | 200–400 Hz build-up across several parts, unfiltered reverb returns | Cut 2–3 dB on pads, guitars and keys there; high-pass the returns; check the arrangement for too many parts in that range |
| Boomy | 60–150 Hz, often one note or one part | Narrow cut on the boomy note, or compress the low band; check kick and bass overlap |
| Thin | Missing 100–300 Hz | Too much high-passing; a gentle low-mid lift; a fuller sound choice |
| Boxy | 400–800 Hz | Broad cut of 2–4 dB |
| Nasal, honky | 800 Hz–1.5 kHz | Broad or medium cut of 2–3 dB |
| Harsh, piercing | 2–5 kHz, often from several bright parts or saturation | Cut on the competing parts rather than the lead; less saturation; dynamic EQ for peaks |
| Sibilant | 5–9 kHz on vocals and cymbals | De-essing (see the vocals sub-skill) |
| Dull, dark | Missing 3–10 kHz | Gentle presence or high shelf lift; a brighter sound choice |
| Brittle, hissy | 8–16 kHz | Gentle high shelf cut; less saturation on bright parts |
| No punch | Attack lost around 2–5 kHz on drums, or transients squashed | Slower compressor attack; transient shaping; less limiting |

## Instruments

| Instrument | Weight or fundamental | Body | Common problem area | Presence and attack | Air |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kick | 40–60 Hz | 60–120 Hz punch | 250–500 Hz boxy | 2–5 kHz beater | — |
| Snare | — | 150–250 Hz | 400–800 Hz ring | 1–5 kHz crack | 5–10 kHz rattle |
| Hi-hats, cymbals | — | — | below 300 Hz (noise) | 3–6 kHz | 8–14 kHz shimmer |
| Bass (synth or bass guitar) | 40–100 Hz | 100–250 Hz | 200–400 Hz mud | 600 Hz–1.5 kHz growl, 2–4 kHz string or pick attack | — |
| 808 | 30–60 Hz | Harmonics 100–500 Hz | — | The attack transient | — |
| Lead vocal | Roughly 80 Hz–1 kHz, depending on the voice and the melody | 150–400 Hz | 200–400 Hz mud, 400–800 Hz boxy, 800 Hz–1.5 kHz nasal | 2–5 kHz presence | 5–9 kHz sibilance, 10–16 kHz air |
| Acoustic guitar | 80–200 Hz boom | Around 200 Hz | 200–400 Hz | 2–5 kHz | 8–12 kHz sparkle |
| Electric guitar | 80–250 Hz | 100–250 Hz | 200–400 Hz, 800 Hz–1.5 kHz honk | 2–5 kHz | Fizz above 6 kHz when distorted |
| Piano | 27.5 Hz (lowest A) to 250 Hz in the low register | 100–400 Hz | 200–500 Hz | 2–5 kHz | 5–10 kHz |
| Pads, chord synths | Wherever the voicing sits; often 150 Hz–5 kHz | — | 200–500 Hz in dense mixes | 1–4 kHz (where they mask vocals) | 8–12 kHz |
| Strings | Depends on the section | 200–500 Hz | 300–600 Hz | 2–6 kHz bow attack | 8–12 kHz |
| Brass | — | 200–800 Hz | 500 Hz–1 kHz | 2–5 kHz bite | — |

For vocals, judge the fundamental from the notes actually sung, not from assumptions about the singer.

## Note frequencies for orientation

| Note | Frequency |
|---|---|
| C1 | 32.7 Hz |
| E1 (lowest note of a standard bass guitar) | 41.2 Hz |
| A1 | 55 Hz |
| C2 | 65.4 Hz |
| A2 | 110 Hz |
| C3 | 130.8 Hz |
| A3 | 220 Hz |
| C4 (middle C) | 261.6 Hz |
| A4 | 440 Hz |

Each octave doubles the frequency. A bass line between E1 and E2 has fundamentals between about 41 and 82 Hz; its harmonics carry it on small speakers.
