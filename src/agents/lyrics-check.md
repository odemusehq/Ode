For each line:

- words: every source word in order, without punctuation. syllables splits its written letters into sung syllable pieces that join back to that word ("beautiful": "beau", "ti", "ful"). stress gives one number per piece: 1 primary, 2 secondary, 0 unstressed. Interpret stress in this line's plausible delivery; a short function word can be stressed deliberately.
- rhyme.sound: your phonetic reading from the ending's last stressed vowel through its coda, preferably IPA. Compare actual intended sounds in this accent, not spelling, word length, a requested scheme or previously assigned letters. Leave it empty when you cannot responsibly choose a reading.
- rhyme.letter: use ONE sound-group alphabet across the ENTIRE song. Assign A to the first partnered sound, B to the next distinct sound, then C, D, and so on in order of first occurrence. Never restart at A at a new section. The same sound keeps its letter wherever it recurs. Thus an opening ABAB followed by two NEW paired sounds becomes CCDD, not AABB. Infer partners from the actual phonetic readings before assigning letters; do not manufacture them to fit an expected pattern.
- rhyme.quality: "perfect" requires the same stressed vowel and all following sounds, with differing preceding onsets. A shared vowel with a different coda, a similar unstressed suffix or similar spelling alone is not a perfect rhyme. Use "near" for an intentional approximate sound relationship. Repeating an identical ending or homophone alone is repetition, not evidence of a new perfect rhyme; it can retain a sound label established by a genuine rhyme elsewhere. A line without a credible partner has letter "", quality "none" and part "".
- rhyme.part: the written letters of the ending that carry the sound, for highlighting, such as "ide" in "inside" or "urning" in "burning". These letters are display anchors, never the evidence used to classify a rhyme.

Consider numbers, contractions, dialect and sung compression. Keep source spelling in syllable pieces instead of replacing digits with invented text. Where a reading is ambiguous, use a plausible reading cautiously; do not label an uncertain pair perfect. Rhyming and speech-like stress are artistic choices. Do not rewrite the lyric, impose rhymes or treat near/no rhyme as an error.
